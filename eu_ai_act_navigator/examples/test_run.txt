===== Final Answer (Explainable) =====
**Summary**: The EU AI Act assigns responsibility for compliance with high-risk AI systems to various parties, including providers, product manufacturers, and deployers.

**Explanation**:

* Providers of high-risk AI systems are responsible for ensuring compliance with the Regulation, including putting in place a quality management system (Article 17).
 * This quality management system must ensure compliance with the Regulation and shall be documented in a systematic and orderly manner (Article 17).
 * Providers must closely cooperate with other high-risk AI system providers and stakeholders to enable compliance (Recital 85).
 * For stand-alone AI systems, the provider is responsible for ensuring that the AI system is designed and developed in such a way that it can be effectively overseen by natural persons during use (Article 14).
 * The oversight measures shall be commensurate with the risks, level of autonomy, and context of use of the high-risk AI system, and shall be ensured through measures identified and built into the high-risk AI system by the provider or implemented by the deployer (Article 14).
 * The provider must also give consideration to whether the high-risk AI system is likely to have an adverse impact on persons under the age of 18 and, as appropriate, other vulnerable groups (Article 9).
 * The product manufacturer of a product that includes a high-risk AI system is responsible for complying with the obligations of the provider established in the Regulation, including ensuring that the AI system embedded in the final product complies with the requirements of the Regulation (Recital 87).
 * Deployers of high-risk AI systems must also be aware of and take into account possible risks and adverse impacts when using the system, including instructions for use provided by the provider (Article 14).
